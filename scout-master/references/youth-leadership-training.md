# Youth leadership training

Use this section when the user asks about training youth leaders, running ILST, preparing a new Senior Patrol Leader, or sending Scouts to NYLT or NAYLE.

## The names, old and new

Troop-level youth leader training has changed names over the years:
- **Junior Leader Training (JLT)**, the older name
- **Troop Leadership Training (TLT)**, used in the 2000s
- **Introduction to Leadership Skills for Troops (ILST)**, the current course (updated edition around 2017)

If the user says "JLT" or "TLT", they mean what is now ILST.

## The youth leadership training path

1. **ILST**, run inside the troop by the Scoutmaster and the SPL. It is the first step and a prerequisite for the next two.
2. **NYLT (National Youth Leadership Training)**, a week-long course run by the council, usually in summer. For Scouts who have completed ILST and are usually at least 13 and First Class (check the council's current rules).
3. **NAYLE (National Advanced Youth Leadership Experience)**, an advanced course at a national high adventure base (such as Philmont), for NYLT graduates.

Scouts who complete ILST can wear the "Trained" emblem on their position patch.

## ILST at a glance

- **Who runs it:** the Scoutmaster with the SPL, helped by previously trained Scouts and trained adults. The SPL should lead as much as possible, because teaching it is itself leadership practice.
- **Who takes it:** every Scout holding a troop leadership position (elected or appointed), at the start of each term, even if they have taken it before. Some troops include all older Scouts. Adults don't take it.
- **When:** soon after elections. If it's delayed more than a few weeks, hold a short leaders' orientation right after elections so new leaders know their jobs.
- **Size:** works best with 6 or more Scouts. Small troops can team up with a nearby troop.
- **Length:** three modules of about 60 to 90 minutes each, plus games.
- **Format options:** one module before each of three troop meetings, one day with breaks, or a weekend campout with modules between activities.
- Use the current official ILST syllabus from scouting.org. It has the full scripts and games.

## The three modules

| Module | Topic | What Scouts learn |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Troop Organization | What each leadership position does, how the troop and PLC work, vision, servant leadership |
| 2 | Tools of Leadership | Communicating, planning, and teaching skills (often taught with the EDGE method: Explain, Demonstrate, Guide, Enable) |
| 3 | Leadership and Teamwork | How teams grow through stages, how a leader's style changes as the team grows, inclusion, vision, ethics and values of a leader |

## The Scoutmaster's jobs

Before:
1. Hold troop elections, then let the SPL appoint the other positions.
2. Meet with the SPL to plan the course together. Give the SPL real sessions to lead.
3. Pick the format and date, reserve the place, gather supplies (flip chart, markers, game materials, position descriptions).
4. Recruit 1 or 2 trained adults or experienced Scouts to help.

During:
- Coach from the side. Let the SPL and trained Scouts lead sessions and games.
- Keep it active. Use games and real troop examples, not lectures.
- Follow Youth Protection rules (two registered adults present).

After:
- Record completion in Scoutbook so Scouts get the Trained emblem.
- Meet each new leader to set expectations for their position (what success looks like, how you'll check in).
- Hold a first PLC meeting soon after to put the training into practice.
- Encourage eligible Scouts to attend NYLT, and help them find a scholarship or troop funding if needed.

## Other quick trainings the Scoutmaster can offer

- **New SPL orientation:** a 30 minute talk right after elections on running meetings and the PLC.
- **Patrol leader handbook review:** go through the Patrol Leader Handbook together.
- **Troop guide training:** how to help new Scouts with their first ranks.
- **Den chief training:** for Scouts who will help a Cub Scout den (the council offers it).

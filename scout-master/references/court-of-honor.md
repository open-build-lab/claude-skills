# Court of Honor

Use this section when the user is planning or running a Court of Honor, training the master of ceremonies, writing a script, or preparing awards.

## What it is

A Court of Honor is a formal, public ceremony to recognize Scouts who earned ranks, merit badges and other awards since the last one. Families and guests are invited. Troops usually hold one every 3 or 4 months. An Eagle Court of Honor is a separate ceremony for one Eagle Scout (see the end of this section).

Scouts should receive their badges quickly, often at the next troop meeting, and then be formally recognized at the Court of Honor. A troop can present the patch at a meeting and the rank card or pin at the Court of Honor, or the other way around.

## Who does what

| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Scoutmaster** | Coaches the SPL and MC, makes sure every Scout earning something is recognized, reviews the script, runs the rehearsal, presents ranks or a Scoutmaster's Minute, thanks volunteers |
| **SPL and PLC** | Plan the program: theme, opening and closing, who presents, special features |
| **Master of ceremonies (MC)** | Usually the SPL or an older Scout. Welcomes guests, keeps the program moving, introduces each part and calls Scouts forward |
| **Advancement chair** (committee) | Runs the advancement report from Scoutbook, buys the badges and cards, sorts awards into labeled envelopes |
| **Committee chair and committee** | Location, budget, refreshments or potluck, invitations to families and guests |
| **Color guard** | Presents and retires the flags |
| **Scribe / historian** | Slideshow of the troop's recent activities, photos at the event |
| **Quartermaster** | Flags, setup, tables, chairs, cleanup |
| **Parents** | Often called up to pin the rank on their Scout |

## Planning timeline

| When | Task | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 6 to 8 weeks before | Set date and place. PLC picks a theme and the MC. | Scoutmaster, SPL, committee chair |
| 4 weeks before | Announce to families. Ask for potluck or refreshment sign-ups. | Committee, SPL |
| 3 weeks before | Cut-off date for advancement to be recorded in Scoutbook | Advancement chair, Scoutmaster |
| 2 weeks before | Run the Scoutbook report, order badges, cards, pins. Assign parts: color guard, presenters, slideshow. | Advancement chair, SPL |
| 1 to 2 weeks before | Write or adapt the script. Scoutmaster reviews it. | MC, SPL, Scoutmaster |
| 1 week before | Rehearsal with MC, color guard and presenters | Scoutmaster, SPL |
| Day before | Awards sorted into envelopes in the order they will be called; check names are spelled right | Advancement chair |
| Event day | Setup 1 hour early, sound check, final walk-through | Quartermaster, MC |
| After | Thank-you notes, photos to families and newsletter, debrief at the next PLC | SPL, Scoutmaster |

## Training the master of ceremonies

The Scoutmaster (or an assistant Scoutmaster) coaches the MC in 2 short sessions.

Session 1, about 30 minutes, 2 weeks before:
- Explain the MC's job: welcome everyone, keep things moving, make every Scout feel recognized.
- Go through the run of show together. Let the MC write it in their own words.
- Teach the basics of speaking: stand up straight, face the audience, speak slowly and loudly, pause for applause, look up from the paper.
- Practice pronouncing every Scout's name. Ask the Scouts or parents if unsure.
- Plan for problems: a Scout who isn't there (say "we'll present it at the next meeting"), a missing award, a microphone that fails, losing your place (pause, smile, look at your notes).

Session 2, the rehearsal, about 45 minutes, 1 week before:
- Full walk-through in the room if possible, with the color guard and presenters.
- Practice the flag ceremony commands ("Color guard, advance", "Audience, please rise", "Color guard, post the colors", "Color guard, retire the colors").
- Practice calling Scouts up, where they stand, and where parents stand for pinning.
- Time it. Aim for 45 to 75 minutes total.
- Give positive feedback first, then one or two things to improve.

Give the MC a cue card version of the script in large type, and a printed list of awardees in order.

## Run of show (sample, about 60 minutes)

```
Before:   Greeters at the door, slideshow running, awards table set
0:00  Call to order by the MC; ask guests to silence phones
0:02  Color guard presents the colors; Pledge of Allegiance
0:05  Opening ceremony: Scout Oath and Law (or candle ceremony)
0:10  Welcome and introduction of guests (MC); thank the chartered organization
0:13  Troop highlights since the last Court of Honor (slideshow or Scout recap)
0:20  New Scouts welcomed (Scout rank)
0:25  Rank advancement: Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class, Star, Life
      (each rank: presenter explains what it means, Scouts called up,
       parents pin the rank, Scouts give parents a mother's/parent's pin)
0:40  Merit badges (grouped by Scout, or call all at once to save time)
0:47  Other awards: leadership positions, Trained emblems, camping or service awards,
      Eagle Palms, adult volunteer thank-yous
0:52  Scoutmaster's Minute
0:55  Announcements: upcoming events (SPL)
0:57  Color guard retires the colors; closing
1:00  Refreshments, photos
```

## Ideas to make it special

- A **candle ceremony** for the Scout Law (12 candles) and Oath, or a stepped candle display for each rank
- A short **skit or demo** by a patrol from a recent outing
- **Older Scouts present ranks** to younger Scouts they mentored
- Projected **rank images** as each rank is called
- Let each Scout earning Star or Life say one sentence about their favorite moment

## Award preparation checklist

- Scoutbook advancement report for all items approved since the last Court of Honor
- Rank patches, rank cards, parent pins, merit badge patches and cards
- Position patches and Trained emblems
- Envelopes labeled with Scout name and items, sorted in calling order
- Spell-checked list of names for the MC
- Spare safety pins

## Eagle Court of Honor

Usually planned by the Eagle Scout and their family with the troop, at a time and place they choose. The Scoutmaster usually:
- Helps the family plan and shares the troop's usual script
- Gives a Scoutmaster charge or a talk about the Scout's journey
- Arranges for the troop to provide the color guard and Scout participants
- Makes sure the Eagle certificate and badge have arrived from the council

Typical parts: an Eagle charge, the Eagle Scout promise, presentation of the Eagle medal to the parents to pin on the Scout, the Scout presenting a mentor pin to someone who helped them, and letters of congratulation.

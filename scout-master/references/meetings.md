# Meetings and planning

## Weekly troop meeting (about 90 minutes)

Use this standard Scouts BSA format. Adjust the times to the troop's meeting length. Every part has a youth leader in charge.

| Time | Part | Led by | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 to 0:15 | Pre-opening | Patrol leader on duty | A game or challenge for Scouts as they arrive |
| 0:15 to 0:20 | Opening | SPL | Flag ceremony, Scout Oath and Scout Law |
| 0:20 to 0:40 | Skills instruction | Troop guide, older Scouts or an adult | Teach one skill, split by experience level if needed (new Scouts versus experienced) |
| 0:40 to 0:55 | Patrol meetings | Patrol leaders | Plan duties for the next outing, menus, practice the skill |
| 0:55 to 1:15 | Inter-patrol activity | ASPL | A game or competition that uses the skill of the night |
| 1:15 to 1:25 | Closing | SPL, then Scoutmaster | Announcements, then the Scoutmaster's Minute, then a closing ceremony |
| 1:25 onward | After the meeting | SPL and Scoutmaster | Clean up, quick check-in on how it went |

Template for output:

```
TROOP MEETING PLAN
Date: ____   Theme / skill of the month: ____
Patrol on duty (setup, flag, cleanup): ____

[table as above with the specific game, skill and activity filled in]

Materials needed: ...
Notes for the SPL: ...
```

Tie the skill instruction to the next outing whenever possible. If the troop camps in October, the September meetings can teach fire building, cooking, knots or first aid that Scouts will use on that trip.

## Patrol Leaders' Council (PLC)

The PLC is the troop's planning team: SPL (chairs it), ASPL, patrol leaders, troop guide, and the scribe. The Scoutmaster attends as a coach and does not run it. It usually meets monthly.

Agenda template:

```
PLC AGENDA, led by the SPL
1. Opening and attendance (scribe takes notes)
2. Patrol reports: how each patrol is doing, who has been missing
3. Review last month: what went well, what to change
4. Next outing: plan, duty roster, gear, menu, who leads
5. Plan the next 4 weekly meetings: skill, game and patrol on duty for each
6. Upcoming events: Court of Honor, service project, fundraiser
7. Scoutmaster's comments (short)
8. Closing
```

## Annual planning

Once a year (often late summer), the PLC builds the troop's calendar. Help them by:
1. Listing fixed dates first: school holidays, summer camp, district and council events, Courts of Honor (often 3 or 4 a year), fundraisers like popcorn sales, the troop committee's dates.
2. Aiming for one outing a month, mixing easy car camping, a cabin trip in winter, a service project, and one bigger adventure.
3. Giving each month a skill theme that prepares for that month's outing.
4. Getting the troop committee to approve the plan and budget.

Output a month-by-month table with columns: Month, Outing, Skill theme, Other events.

## Scoutmaster's Minute

A 1 to 2 minute talk at the end of a meeting that teaches a value through a short story or object lesson. It usually ties to a point of the Scout Law. Write it to be spoken out loud: simple words, one image or story, one clear message, ending with a line Scouts can remember. Avoid preaching.

# Outings, campouts and trips

Read `safety.md` too before finalizing any outing plan.

## Campout plan template

```
CAMPOUT PLAN: [name / location]
Dates: ____   Depart: ____ from ____   Return: ____
Youth in charge: SPL ____, trip leader ____
Adults (at least 2 registered, YPT-current): ____
Estimated cost per Scout: ____

GOAL / SKILLS: what Scouts will learn or advance on

SCHEDULE
Friday: arrive, set up camp by patrol, cracker barrel, lights out
Saturday: breakfast by patrol, morning activity, lunch, afternoon activity,
          dinner, campfire program, lights out
Sunday: breakfast, Scouts' Own service (optional), pack out, leave no trace check, depart

DUTY ROSTER (per patrol): cook, assistant cook, water/fire, cleanup, rotate each meal
GEAR: troop gear and personal gear (see lists below)
MENU: per patrol, with shopping list and budget
SAFETY: first aid kit and trained adult, weather plan, nearest hospital,
        buddy system, permission slips and health forms collected
```

## Personal gear list (adjust for season)

Scout Basic Essentials: pocketknife (if they have their Totin' Chip), first aid kit, extra clothing, rain gear, water bottle, flashlight, trail food, matches or fire starters, sun protection, map and compass.

Plus: sleeping bag rated for the weather, sleeping pad, mess kit and utensils, toiletries, Scout handbook, uniform (Class A for travel if the troop does that), and medications handed to the adult in charge.

Cold weather: layers (no cotton), warm hat, gloves, extra socks, a warmer bag or liner.

## Troop / patrol gear list

Tents, dining fly or tarp, patrol stove and fuel, cook kit, cooler, water jugs, lantern, cleaning supplies (three-pot dishwashing), trash bags, axe/saw kit (with trained users only), first aid kit, rope.

## Menus

Patrols plan their own menus, so offer a sample and a shopping list the patrol can adapt. Plan by meal: number of Scouts, simple recipes, ask about allergies and dietary needs (vegetarian, halal, nut allergies), and budget per Scout. A typical budget is set by the troop. Include a food safety note (keep coolers cold, wash hands).

## Types of outings

- **Car camping**: easiest, good for new Scouts and first campouts.
- **Cabin camping**: good for winter.
- **Day trips**: hikes, bike rides, museum visits, service projects.
- **Summer camp**: a week at a council camp, a big chance for merit badges.
- **High adventure**: older Scouts, usually 13 or 14 and up, at places like Philmont (backpacking), Northern Tier (canoeing), Florida Sea Base (sailing, snorkeling) or Summit Bechtel Reserve. These take 12 to 18 months to plan, plus fundraising and fitness training. Check each base's current age and fitness rules.
- **District and council events**: Klondike Derby (winter sled competition), camporees.

## Before and after

Before: permission slips and annual health forms (Parts A and B for most outings, Part C for long or strenuous trips), driver list, tour or activity plan if the council requires one, weather check two days ahead.

After: the PLC reviews what went well and what to change. Record nights camped, hikes and service hours so they count for advancement.

# Communication

## Rules for any message

- Messages to Scouts go to the group or include a parent or another adult. Never write a private one-to-one message from an adult to a Scout (Youth Protection).
- Lead with what parents need to do and by when (RSVP, pay, sign the form, pack).
- Keep it short, friendly and skimmable. Use dates with the day of the week.
- Don't share Scouts' health information, and only share full names and photos where the troop has permission.

## Parent email template (outing)

```
Subject: [Outing] on [Date]: sign up by [Deadline]

Hi Troop families,

Our Scouts are heading to [place] on [dates]. The [patrol / SPL] planned this trip,
and Scouts will [main activity / skills].

What you need to do by [deadline]:
1. RSVP here: [link]
2. Pay $[amount] via [method]
3. Make sure your Scout's health form is current

Key details
- Leave: [day, time, place]   Return: [day, time, place]
- Bring: [short gear list or link]
- Drivers needed: [number]. Reply if you can help.

Questions? Reply to this email.

Yours in Scouting,
[Name], Scoutmaster
```

## Monthly newsletter outline

1. A short welcome from the Scoutmaster (2 or 3 sentences)
2. Recap of last month, with a highlight story led by Scouts
3. Upcoming events with dates
4. Advancement shout-outs (ranks and merit badges)
5. Skill of the month
6. Volunteer asks from the troop committee
7. Reminders (dues, forms, uniform)

## Recruiting and asking for help

- Recruiting: for open houses and working with Cub Scout packs, read `open-house-recruiting.md`.
- Volunteers: ask for one specific job with a clear time commitment ("drive on Saturday 9 to 11 am") rather than general help. Remind parents that many roles need no outdoor skills, like committee member, treasurer, or merit badge counselor.

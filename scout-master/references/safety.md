# Safety and Youth Protection

These are Scouting America's core safety rules. They change from time to time, so for anything exact, tell the user to check the current **Guide to Safe Scouting** and Youth Protection policies at scouting.org.

## Youth Protection (Scouting's barriers to abuse)

- **Two registered adults** are present at all Scouting activities, including meetings. One of them must be 21 or older. When there are female Scouts, a registered female adult over 21 must be present.
- **No one-on-one contact** between an adult and a Scout, in person, by phone or online. A Scoutmaster conference or merit badge meeting happens in view of other people. Messages to a Scout include a parent or another leader.
- **Youth Protection Training (YPT)** is required for every registered adult and must stay current (it is renewed every 2 years). Adults who stay overnight on a campout must be registered and trained.
- **Separate sleeping**: adults and youth do not share tents, except a parent with their own child. Youth tent mates are close in age (no more than about 2 years apart).
- **Privacy** when changing and showering, and appropriate clothing.
- **No hazing, bullying or initiations.**
- **Report** any suspected abuse to the authorities right away, then to the Scout executive. Leaders are mandatory reporters in Scouting.

## Outings

- Permission from parents for each outing, and an **Annual Health and Medical Record** for each participant. Parts A and B for most outings, Part C (a physical exam) for events over 72 hours or high adventure.
- **Buddy system** for everyone.
- Check the weather and have a plan for storms and lightning.
- Bring a first aid kit and have an adult trained in first aid. Note the nearest hospital.
- Drivers: licensed, insured, seat belt for everyone, follow council travel rules.
- **Leave No Trace** principles.

## Activities that need extra training or rules

Look these up in the Guide to Safe Scouting before planning: swimming and boating (Safe Swim Defense and Safety Afloat, with a trained adult), climbing and rappelling, shooting sports, using woods tools (Totin' Chip), fire (Firem'n Chit), high adventure, and any activity listed as not allowed. Flag these in any plan and say what training is needed.

## When a request goes against the rules

Say so kindly and give the safe alternative. For example: "Scouting rules don't allow a one-on-one meeting with a Scout. You can hold the conference at the side of the room during the troop meeting, where other adults can see you."

# Advancement

The ranks, in order: Scout, Tenderfoot, Second Class, First Class, Star, Life, Eagle. After Eagle, Scouts can earn Eagle Palms. Advancement is tracked in Scoutbook. Requirements change from time to time, so tell the user to check the current Scouts BSA Handbook or Scoutbook for exact requirement wording.

Key idea: advancement is a tool for growth, not a race. Each Scout moves at their own pace. A requirement is done when the Scout has done it as written, not more and not less.

## Steps for each rank

1. The Scout learns and is tested on the requirements (signed off by a leader the Scoutmaster has approved).
2. **Scoutmaster conference**.
3. **Board of review** by the troop committee.
4. Recognized at the next **Court of Honor**.

## Scoutmaster conference

A friendly talk between the Scoutmaster and the Scout. It is not a test or a re-check of skills. Its purpose is to get to know the Scout, celebrate progress and set goals. It must follow Youth Protection rules: held where other people can see you, never fully private one-on-one.

For full rank-by-rank questions (Scout to Eagle), adapting by age and position, and what to do if a Scout isn't ready, read `scoutmaster-conference.md`.

## Board of review

Three to six troop committee members, not the Scoutmaster, assistant Scoutmasters or the Scout's own parent or guardian. It checks that the Scout had a good experience and looks at how the troop is doing. It should not re-test skills. If the Scout isn't approved, the board explains what to work on.

## Merit badges

The Scout talks to the Scoutmaster and gets the name of a registered merit badge counselor, works with the counselor (always with Youth Protection in mind, so no one-on-one), and the counselor signs off in Scoutbook or on the blue card. Help the user suggest badges that fit an upcoming outing or summer camp, and track which Eagle-required badges a Scout still needs.

## Eagle Scout

Requirements include the ranks before it, a set number of merit badges with specific Eagle-required ones, a leadership position for a set time as a Life Scout, a service project the Scout plans and leads, references, a written statement of ambitions, a Scoutmaster conference and an Eagle board of review. The Scout has to finish everything before turning 18. Check current rules for exact numbers.

How the Scoutmaster helps:
- Meet with Life Scouts early to map a timeline back from the 18th birthday.
- Encourage a project that benefits a group other than Scouting, and that the Scout leads others on, not just does alone.
- Point them to the official Eagle Scout Service Project Workbook and the council or district Eagle advisor.

Output idea: a timeline table with columns Requirement, Target date, Status.

## Positions of responsibility

Star, Life and Eagle need time in a leadership position (SPL, ASPL, patrol leader, troop guide, quartermaster, scribe, historian, librarian, chaplain aide, instructor, den chief, outdoor ethics guide, webmaster and others). Help the Scoutmaster explain what each one does and set clear expectations at the start.

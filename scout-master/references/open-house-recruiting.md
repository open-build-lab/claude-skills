# Open house and recruiting

Use this section when the user is planning an open house, a recruiting night, a school or community event, or working with Cub Scout packs to bring in new Scouts.

## Who you are trying to reach

- Cub Scouts finishing Arrow of Light (5th grade), who usually cross over to a troop in spring
- Youth ages 11 to 17 who have never been in Scouting, and their parents
- Friends of current Scouts (often the best recruits)

Parents decide too, so plan for both: excitement for the kids, and clear answers for the parents (cost, time, safety, who the leaders are).

## The Scoutmaster's role

The Scoutmaster works with the troop's membership chair, the committee and the SPL. Typical jobs:
- Set the date and plan with the membership chair and SPL
- Invite families personally (letter or email), and call or message them the day before
- Speak to parents about the troop's program, safety and costs
- Close the event with a Scoutmaster's Minute
- Make sure new Scouts have a great first activity soon after joining

The Scouts should run the show: the SPL emcees, and Scouts staff the demo stations, greet guests and lead games.

## Timeline (adjust to your season)

| When | What | Who |
|---|---|---|
| 6 to 8 weeks before | Pick the date, reserve the place, plan stations | Scoutmaster, membership chair, SPL |
| 4 to 6 weeks before | Visit Cub packs and dens, school talks or flyers where allowed, post on social media and community calendars, ask Scouts to invite friends | Scoutmaster, den chiefs, Scouts |
| 2 weeks before | Send personal invitations to interested families | Scoutmaster |
| 1 day before | Reminder call or message to families | Scoutmaster or membership chair |
| Event day | Open house | Whole troop |
| Within 1 week | Follow-up with every family | Membership chair, Scoutmaster |
| Within 1 month | Exciting first activity for new Scouts | PLC, troop guide |

## Run of show (about 90 minutes)

```
0:00  Early arrival game led by Scouts; greeters at the door, name tags, sign-in sheet
0:10  Opening: flag ceremony, welcome by the SPL
0:15  Demo stations (youth rotate in small groups, led by Scouts)
0:50  Parent session in a separate area while youth stay at stations
      (Scoutmaster and committee chair: what the troop does, calendar,
       safety and Youth Protection, costs, how parents can help)
1:10  Everyone together: short slideshow or video of troop adventures
1:20  How to join: application, fees, next meeting date, first outing
1:25  Scoutmaster's Minute and closing; refreshments
```

## Demo station ideas

Pick 4 to 6 stations, each run by 2 or 3 Scouts, 5 to 10 minutes each. Make them hands-on.
- **Tent pitching race** or set up a dining fly
- **Knots and lashing:** tie a square knot, or build a tripod or flagpole with lashings
- **Camp cooking:** Dutch oven cobbler, foil packs or s'mores (with fire safety)
- **First aid:** splints and bandages, "treat the patient" challenge
- **Map and compass / geocaching** mini course
- **Model rocket** or STEM demo
- **Gear display:** what to pack for a campout, with a backpack to try on
- **Photo wall** of past trips, summer camp and high adventure, with patrol flags
- **Advancement wall:** the ranks from Scout to Eagle, so families see the path

Avoid anything that needs special training without a trained adult (for example shooting sports or climbing).

## Parent talking points

- What Scouts BSA is: youth-led, patrol method, outdoors, leadership, character
- Troop calendar highlights: monthly outings, summer camp, high adventure
- Safety: Youth Protection Training for all adults, two registered adults at every activity, the Guide to Safe Scouting
- Costs: registration fee, troop dues, uniform, handbook, summer camp, and help available if cost is a problem
- How parents help: committee roles, drivers, merit badge counselors. Many roles need no outdoor skills.
- How to join: online at beascout.scouting.org or a paper application

## Working with Cub Scout packs

- Send older Scouts as **den chiefs** to help an Arrow of Light den all year.
- Invite Arrow of Light dens to a troop meeting or a joint campout.
- Offer to run the **crossover ceremony** at the pack's Blue and Gold banquet.
- Leave a simple flyer with the troop's meeting night, contact and next event.

## Follow-up (where most recruiting succeeds or fails)

- Contact every family within a few days, answer questions and invite them to the next meeting.
- Put new Scouts in a new-Scout patrol with a troop guide.
- Start Scout rank right away so they feel progress.
- Plan an exciting first outing within a month, and encourage summer camp.
- Check in with new parents after a month.

## Output templates

When the user asks for an open house plan, give: the timeline table, the run of show with names of roles, the station list with who runs each, a supply list, and a short invitation message for families.
